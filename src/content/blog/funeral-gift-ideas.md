  <div class="how-to-choose">
    <h2 class="section-title">How to Choose the Right Funeral Gift</h2>
    <ul class="tips-list">
      <li class="tip-item"><strong>Consider culture and faith:</strong> Some families prefer donations or food, while others avoid flowers. When in doubt, ask a close friend of the family.</li>
      <li class="tip-item"><strong>Keep it simple:</strong> Choose modest, useful items in neutral colors, and avoid strong fragrances or anything that creates extra work.</li>
      <li class="tip-item"><strong>Time your gift:</strong> Gifts are welcome before, during, or after the service. A few weeks later can be especially meaningful when support tapers off.</li>
      <li class="tip-item"><strong>Pair with a note:</strong> A few sincere lines—“I’m here for you”—often matter more than the gift itself. If you need help, try our <a href="/blog/sympathy-card-messages">sympathy message guide</a>.</li>
      <li class="tip-item"><strong>Offer practical help:</strong> Add a personal voucher for a ride, a chore, or a homemade meal to accompany a small gift.</li>
    </ul>

    <h2 class="section-title">Etiquette and Thoughtful Timing</h2>
    <p class="intro-text">According to the <a href="https://emilypost.com/advice/funeral-etiquette" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Emily Post Institute</a>, sympathy gestures should be modest and respectful; handwritten messages and charitable contributions are always appropriate. <a href="https://www.funeralwise.com/etiquette/sympathy-gifts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Funeralwise</a> notes that sending a gift or card shortly after the death is common, but support offered weeks later can be particularly comforting.</p>
    <p class="intro-text">If the obituary requests donations instead of flowers, honor that wish and include a small, practical item (like a candle or card) if you still want to give something tangible.</p>
